## 2. Conversation with User

- **Name:** Tapon Sahu
- **Role:** Mess Staff / Kitchen Coordinator, BH-7 Mess, NIT Silchar

### Three Specific Things He Told Me

1. **Prep quantities come from muscle memory and day-of-week trends.** He uses a baseline of 120g to 130g raw rice per student (26 to 28 kg for about 200 students) and 170g to 180g dressed chicken per non-veg student. Turnout is higher on Sundays.
2. **Evening snacks and breakfast boiled eggs are the hardest to predict.** If he fries 200 Aloo Chops and 250 students show up, they run out in about 10 minutes. If he fries 250 and students go out for tea instead, the extra chops go soggy as leftovers. Fryums also run out fast because students grab 4 to 5 pieces at once.
3. **Edible leftovers are not thrown away.** Leftover cooked rice (for example, the 2.5 kg lunch surplus) is covered and reused in next-day jeera or fried rice, or eaten by kitchen helpers. Leftover dry snacks (Aloo Chops, puris) go to night security guards, cleaning staff, or stray animals.

### One Thing That Surprised Me

I expected mess staff to overcook and dump pots of food every night. According to Tapon Sahu, kitchen pot waste is under 2%, because the contractor's budget depends on it. He said student plate waste in the buffet setup (taking 4 to 5 scoops and dumping half into the disposal drum) is roughly 10 times higher than kitchen production waste. These are his estimates, not figures I measured.

## 3. Data Collection Method (`data.csv`)

`data.csv` contains 38 meal-by-meal entries collected over 3 days (`2026-11-13` to `2026-11-15`) directly from the BH-7 daily kitchen register and serving desk logs.

**Fields recorded per entry:**

- `prepared_qty`: quantity prepared
- `students_served`: number of students served
- `leftover_qty`: quantity left over
- `shortage_refill_qty`: quantity added to cover a shortage

**Recording spot:** each entry also notes where it was recorded, either `BH-7 Mess kitchen counter` or `BH-7 Mess serving desk`.
